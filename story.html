The story

Why Rare
Threads exists.

Mbali Shongwe grew up in a township without many open doors. Rare Threads is him building one himself — with a sewing machine, an idea, and a story worth telling.

The founder

Soweto roots.
Diepsloot present.

Mbali grew up in Soweto and creates today from Diepsloot — "intwana yase kasi," a kasi boy through and through. As a child he loved soccer, drawing, and style — always finding a way to make something his own.

He describes himself simply: "a rare breed, always begged to differ."

Where it started

From cutting clothes
to making them.

Before high school, Mbali started experimenting — cutting and modifying his own clothes, including deliberately ripping them. Eventually a question followed naturally: why not make clothing from scratch?

His first full piece was a tracksuit, made before he fully understood where the idea would take him. He describes the creativity as something that "started in mind" — closer to a calling than a hobby.

"I feel like myself" — that's how he describes what it's like to create, and he says he still sometimes surprises himself with what he manages to make.

Rare isn't hard to find. It's who you already are.
The Rare Threads philosophy
What "rare" means

The definition of Rare.

Rare can mean hard to find. Mbali's deeper interpretation is unique — clothing built around individuality, not imitation. He wants every piece to feel like it defines the person wearing it and tells a story about them, rather than following whatever's trending.

Confidence

Wearing something that's actually yours, not borrowed from a trend.

Individuality

Design built around a person's own story, not a repeated print.

Culture

Kasi life as lived identity — language, music, community, resilience.

Belief

"Dreams never die while the owner of the dream exist."

Behind the threads

Where the creative process actually happens.

Not a studio. Not a factory. A home workspace, a sewing machine, and handwork where it's needed. That's the honest starting point — and it's part of what makes each piece what it is.

Some artwork and drawings used on garments are developed by Mbali; others come from collaborating artists working from his direction. Not every piece is claimed as hand-drawn by Mbali personally — the ideas are his; the execution is sometimes shared.

Where this is going

The dream is bigger than a home workspace.

Mbali's ambition is a recognised South African brand, his own workspace and eventually a shop, and — in time — a worldwide audience. As it grows, the goal is to create opportunities for other creatives from the kasi too: artists, photographers, designers, makers. None of that exists yet. This is the beginning of it.
